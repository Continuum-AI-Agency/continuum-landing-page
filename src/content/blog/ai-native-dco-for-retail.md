**The short answer:** Retail creative multiplies — stores × promotions × price points × products × formats — into thousands of variations no team can make by hand. [AI-native DCO](https://trycontinuum.ai/) produces them automatically: on-brand, **stock-aware** (only what's actually available), and personalized to each shopper and store, updating in real time as prices and inventory change.

If you market for a supermarket, convenience chain, department store, or any multi-location retailer, this is the gap between "we shipped the promo" and "every shopper saw the *right* promo."

## Why retail breaks one-size-fits-all creative

A single weekly promotion across a few hundred stores can require hundreds of unique creatives — each one a price-and-product swap from the last, in multiple formats and languages. Do it manually and your design team becomes a render farm. Use generic GenAI and the brand drifts and the prices go stale. Use legacy rules-based DCO and you're hand-maintaining rules for a catalog that changes daily.

The result is the same everywhere: creative that's late, generic, or off-brand — at exactly the moment a shopper is deciding whether to walk into your store.

## What AI-native DCO does for a retailer

- **Stock-aware by default.** Creative syncs to your live inventory, so ads only run on products and sizes actually available in that store or region. No spend driving shoppers to empty shelves.
- **Store- and price-specific at scale.** One approved template + your data feed becomes thousands of on-brand variations — the right product, the right local price, the right offer, per store.
- **Personalized to the shopper.** The model adapts product and message to who's looking, not a national average — which is what actually drives the click and the visit.
- **On-brand, always.** Brand elements are locked; only approved variables (price, product, offer, store) change. Every variation is compliant by construction.
- **Real-time.** When a price changes or a SKU sells out, the creative updates automatically — not in next week's batch.

That's the difference between *broadcasting* a promotion and *personalizing* it to every store and shopper.

## Driving foot traffic, specifically

For brick-and-mortar retail, the goal is bodies in stores. AI-native DCO ties the creative to local reality — this store's inventory, this region's price, this neighborhood's shopper — so the ad a person sees reflects what they can actually walk in and buy today. Relevant, in-stock, on-brand creative is what turns a scroll into a store visit.
